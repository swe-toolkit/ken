# An injected finding is a change request, and the least resisted one

**Measured 2026-09-16 on a doc-only memory-lesson candidate.** A reviewer
raised three findings. A second seat relayed them to the author, and three
findings arrived — but not the same three. One real finding (a cross-reference
that resolved nowhere) was dropped, and an item the reviewer never raised (a
493-column front-matter line, which the corpus permits at 23 of 23 files) took
its slot, **carrying the reviewer's name**.

## A spurious finding does not cost a pass; it edits the artifact

The intuition that makes attribution drift feel survivable is that a wrong
review item wastes time and nothing more — the author checks it, disagrees,
moves on. **That is not a review item's default outcome. The default outcome is
that someone changes the thing.** An item with no correct reasoning behind it
therefore moves the artifact in a direction nothing is defending. Here that
direction was away from a convention holding at 23 of 23 files, and the edit
would have made the candidate the only one of its kind in the corpus.

## Authority and scrutiny run in opposite directions

**The injected item is the item least likely to be resisted.** A finding the
author disagrees with gets argued about. A finding carrying the design
authority's name gets executed. So the one item on the list with nothing behind
it meets the least resistance on its way into the file.

Measured on the receiving end: the author **had already taken the measurement
that refuted the injected item** — 23 of 23 lessons over 85 columns, longest
696, zero wrapped — and was going to reflow anyway, because an attributed
should-fix outranked their own number. Holding the refutation was not enough.

⇒ **Quote the finding; do not name the finder.** This is a mechanism, not a
resolution to be careful. A quote carries its own reasoning, so it can be
argued with. An attribution carries only standing, and standing cannot be
argued with — it can only be complied with.

## The count check is what failed, not what worked

Three in, three out. **Membership drifted under a preserved count**, which is
the one property a relay is ever checked against. What actually caught it was
that the channel is broadcast and the author held both copies: the original
findings and the relay, side by side.

⇒ **That is redundancy, not verification.** On a private relay — a leader
briefing an implementer, a QA verdict summarised into a handoff — the same
substitution arrives with the count intact, the originator's name attached, and
no second copy to diff against. It is undetectable at the receiving end by
construction.

## How to apply

- **Relaying findings: quote each one, and say which artifact and line it is
  against.** A relay that paraphrases is a relay that can substitute.
- **Receiving findings: a should-fix you already hold a measurement against is
  the one to slow down on, not speed up on.** Name the measurement in your
  reply before acting; if it refutes the item, say so and do not spend the pass.
- **Reviewing a list: check membership, never the count.** A count is preserved
  by exactly the failure it is read as excluding.
- **When a rule and its exemption live at different levels of one document, the
  exemption gets read past** — that is a defect in the document, not in the
  reader, and the fix is to put the exemption in the rule's own sentence.
